# Chapter 6 — Bring the Questions, Not the Answers

*Draft — first pass. Closes Act 1. Lightly revised 2026-09-11 to fix continuity with the corrected incident (chapters 1–5).*

---

She called Kalu first, because whatever she did next needed to be sanctioned before it happened, not apologised for afterward.

"I want to bring in a second investigator," she told him. "Full access, same terms as mine."

"Ashworth's expecting a name within the week, Ms Reyes. Not a bigger team."

"Ashworth's expecting a name because that's what boards ask for on day two of a crisis. I need you to help her want the right thing on day nine instead." She let that sit for a second. "I found a document last night. Somebody predicted this failure, in writing, eleven months ago, with a working reproduction, and was told to make it lighter-weight and revisit later. Whatever happened to that patient started well before Sam Ilyas touched a keyboard."

Silence on the line, long enough that she wondered if she'd overplayed it.

"Send it to me," Kalu said, quietly. "Not the board. Me, first."

"I will. But that's exactly why this stopped being a one-person job somewhere around midnight, Mr Kalu. If a warning like that got buried once, in one system, it can be buried again, in any of the others — and I don't know yet how many others there are. I have eleven days to tell you whether this was one mistake or a pattern, and I can't chase that across your entire estate on my own in the time I've got. I want a second investigator, and I want it to be someone who's found this exact shape of problem before, somewhere else. Marcus Kwan. On-site by Thursday."

"I don't know the name."

"You will. He's expensive, he's difficult, and he is the only person I trust to stop me finding an answer I like too early." She heard herself say it and recognised, with some discomfort, how true it was.

Kalu exhaled, the sound of a man doing a different kind of arithmetic than the one from the boardroom. "I'll get it signed off. Eleven days is still eleven days, Ms Reyes."

"I know exactly how many days I have," Maya said. "I'd like to spend them finding out what actually happened, rather than finding out what's convenient to have happened. Those aren't always the same use of a week."

---

She texted Marcus after she'd hung up, because some conversations went better in writing, and this was one of them — the kind where she wanted him to have time to say no before he had time to feel obliged to say yes.

*I need you. It's bigger than one incident. Can you be free Thursday.*

The reply came back in under a minute, which for Marcus Kwan was practically enthusiasm.

*Where.*

*Meridian Health Group. Healthcare. A patient's in coronary care because a system nobody was watching quietly stopped working. I'll explain properly when you're here. Bring the questions, not the answers.*

*I only ever bring the questions,* he wrote back. *You know that.*

*I'm counting on it.*

She put the phone face-down on the desk and looked, for a while, at nothing in particular — the dark window, her own reflection in it, tired around the eyes in a way she didn't remember earning. Downstairs, the building was quiet in the specific way an office goes quiet the night after bad news, when everyone who can leave has left and the people who can't are pretending to work.

She thought about Ruth Okafor's document, unread for eleven months in a folder nobody had reason to open until a man nearly died. She thought about Sam, apologising to a desk. She thought about Felix, fluent and proud in front of a system that had never put a single patient at risk, and almost entirely at sea in front of the one that had.

And she thought, not for the first time this week and with considerably less comfort than usual, that she recognised every single piece of this except the ending — because the ending, this time, she hadn't written yet.

Thursday, she decided. She'd know a great deal more by Thursday.

She had no idea, yet, how much of it she wouldn't like.
