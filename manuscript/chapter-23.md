# Chapter 23 — Small, on Purpose

*Draft — first pass. Short, quiet chapter, deliberately — the grace note before the closing chapter, per `writing-plan.md` §4b.*

---

Tariq showed her the tool almost by accident, on her last proper day at Meridian, angling his phone across the desk without quite meeting her eye first, the way people do when they're prouder of something than they're comfortable admitting.

"It's nothing," he said, which was, Maya had come to learn over five weeks, always the thing he said immediately before showing her something that mattered to him.

It was a small app — plain, grey, no logo, the kind of thing that had clearly never once been shown to a designer. A log of numbers. A gentle reminder, twice a day, in a slightly clumsy font he'd apparently never bothered to fix.

"My mum's diabetic. Type 2, diagnosed a few years back. She kept forgetting her readings, or writing them on whatever paper was nearest and losing it before her appointment, and the apps that exist for this are either trying to sell her three subscriptions or want to sync with a smart scale she doesn't own and never will." A small shrug, the specific embarrassed pride of someone who'd solved a real problem for exactly one person and never told anyone at work. "So I built her this. Took me two evenings. She's used it every day for fourteen months. It doesn't do anything clever. It just does the one thing, reliably, for the one person it's for, and I know exactly why every part of it works, because I wrote every part of it myself, slowly, for someone I love."

"You could have used an agent for that in about ten minutes."

"I know. I didn't want to, for this one. I wanted to actually make it." He said it simply, without needing her to understand why, which was, Maya thought, its own kind of answer to a question she hadn't finished asking herself yet. "Everything else I build this year is going to be for a system with forty thousand users and a risk register and six other teams touching it before I'm done. This one's just for her. I think that's why I still like doing it."

Maya looked at the small, plain, slightly ugly app for a moment longer than it probably deserved, and found she liked it more than almost anything else she'd seen in this building.

---

Marcus took her to Shun that evening, for what both of them understood, without needing to say it, was the last time he'd be taking her there for this particular reason.

Aiko didn't ask how the case had gone. She never did, Maya had noticed — she asked what they wanted to eat, and let whatever else needed saying arrive in its own time, the way everything in this kitchen arrived in its own time.

"You look different than the first night you sat here," Aiko said, eventually, wiping the counter in front of Maya, though there was nothing on it. "Less like someone waiting to be told the answer. More like someone who's found out she already had most of it and just needed somewhere quiet enough to hear herself think it."

"I had good teachers."

"You had one good teacher and one good kitchen," Aiko said, and something in the way she said it made clear she considered these different, equally necessary things. "The kitchen only ever does one job. It shows people what happens when you refuse to let something get bigger than the people responsible for it can actually hold. It can't make anyone believe that outside these eight seats. That part was always going to be yours to do."

Yuki set two small plates in front of them, unbidden, unremarkable, exactly as good as everything else that had come before it all evening.

"Will you come back," Aiko asked, "once there's no case to bring you here."

Maya considered it, and found the answer arrived without needing to be searched for. "Yes," she said. "I think I will."

"Good," Aiko said, already turning back to the pass, already somewhere else in her evening. "Ichigo ichie doesn't mean once. It means *fully*, every time. There's a difference, and it took me twenty years to stop confusing the two. You've got a head start on me. Don't waste it."
