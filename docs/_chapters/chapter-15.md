Kalu called her up before she'd finished her coffee, which by now she recognised as the specific rhythm of a bad morning arriving early to get ahead of her.

"Felix has requested a session with Ashworth this afternoon. He wants to present preliminary findings."

"Felix doesn't have findings. I have findings."

"He has a version of them. He's had two days of your interim notes, which I gave him because your terms said full cooperation and I took that to cut both ways." A pause that told her he already regretted it. "I think he's frightened, Ms Reyes. I think he's looked at what's coming and decided the safest thing he can do is be the one who names a cause before anyone names him as one."

Six days left. She did the arithmetic without wanting to — eleven had become six sometime while she was busy learning things she hadn't wanted to know — and understood, with the particular calm that came right before she got genuinely angry, that she wasn't going to make that meeting in time to stop it from starting.

She made it in time to stop it from finishing.

---

Felix was already at the whiteboard when she walked in, and Ashworth was already listening to him in the specific, patient way of a woman deciding whether what she was hearing was a solution or a liability with better posture.

"—so while the investigation's ongoing," Felix was saying, "I think it's important the board sees we're not waiting passively. The pattern points fairly clearly to a junior engineer working outside his experience level, insufficiently supervised on a change he wasn't ready to make alone. That's addressable. That's a staffing and process fix we can announce this week, which buys the investigation the room it needs without—"

"Sam Ilyas did exactly what this organisation trained him to do," Maya said, from the doorway, and watched both of them turn. "He used the tools he was given, the way he was told to use them, on code that six people across four years had already made unreadable, reviewed by a process that takes four minutes because nobody's got longer, using a shared skill template that used to warn him not to do exactly what he did — until eight edits, by six different people, quietly removed the warning, because it was slowing people down. Ruth Okafor told this company, in writing, with a working reproduction, eleven months before any of that happened, and was told her risk framing was difficult to action against delivery deadlines. If you fire Sam this week, Felix, you will have found a name. You will not have found the cause, and the cause will still be sitting in this building on Monday, waiting for the next Sam."

Silence, of a kind Maya was starting to recognise as her favourite sound in this building — the sound of someone hearing something they couldn't immediately find a comfortable place to put.

"That's..." Felix looked between her and Ashworth. "That's a lot more than a staffing issue."

"Yes."

Ashworth hadn't moved. "You have six days, Ms Reyes."

"I know exactly how many I have. I'd like to spend the ones I've got left telling you the truth, rather than spending today telling you a name that photographs well and then spending the next six pretending I believe it myself." Maya set her folder on the table, unopened — she didn't need it, she'd carried every line of it in her head for a week now. "I can give you the full picture in six days. I cannot give you a scapegoat and the full picture in the same week, because they're not the same document, and I'm not willing to write the first one just because it's due sooner."

Ashworth looked at her for a long moment — the particular look, Maya was beginning to understand, of a woman doing arithmetic she genuinely didn't enjoy, weighing a board meeting against something that might actually cost her more if she got it wrong twice.

"Six days," Ashworth said again, quieter. "Don't waste them being right in a room where nobody's listening yet. Bring me something I can act on, not just something I have to believe."

It wasn't agreement. It wasn't refusal either. Maya took it for what it was — the first door Ashworth had left open all week that wasn't already half-closed — and decided, walking out past Felix's still-loaded whiteboard, that it would have to be enough.
