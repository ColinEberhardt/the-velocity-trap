This time it wasn't six people in a room with the blinds down. It was the full board, eleven of them, four in person and seven on a screen that made everyone's face slightly the wrong colour, and a general sense in the room that patience — the specific, expensive kind boards extend to a crisis in its opening week — had quietly run out sometime over the weekend.

"Ms Reyes." The man who spoke first wasn't Ashworth — a non-executive director Maya hadn't met, silver-haired, the particular confidence of someone used to rooms deferring to him before he'd finished a sentence. "We've had your interim brief. Systemic causes, organisational drift, tooling governance. All very thorough. What we have not had — despite Mr Adeyemi's own attempt to give us one earlier this week — is a name."

"Because I don't have one that would survive contact with what actually happened."

"You have Mr Ilyas."

"Mr Ilyas did his job exactly as he was trained to do it, using tools he was told to use, on code six people had already made unreadable, reviewed in four minutes by a process built for that speed and no other, using a shared safety instruction that had been quietly stripped of its own caution eight edits before he ever touched it. If you fire him, you will have produced a statement. You will not have produced an answer, and the next Sam is already in this building, doing the same job, under the same pressure, this afternoon."

"Then Mr Whitfield. He manages the team. The responsibility sits with him."

Maya had known this question was coming since the door opened, and had spent the walk over deciding, with some discomfort, that the honest answer and the safe answer were the same shape of trap from two different directions.

"Josh Whitfield declined a request eleven months ago to add a mandatory reviewer to changes like the one that failed, citing delivery pressure his own leadership had set for him. Felix Adeyemi approved a budget increase four months ago based entirely on velocity numbers, because velocity numbers were the only thing anyone had ever asked either of them to report on. I can find you a decision one of them made that looks bad in isolation. I can find you five. What I can't find you, anywhere in four months of records, is a single point where anyone above them told them output speed mattered less than what that speed was costing in understanding — and until somebody says that sentence and means it, firing the people who never heard it changes nothing except who's afraid to say the next hard thing out loud."

Silence, and then a second voice, from the screen, gentler but no less pointed. "Ms Reyes, I don't doubt your rigour. I doubt whether this board has six days left to spend on rigour. We have a regulator, a press cycle, and a share price that has already noticed we haven't said anything definitive. What would you have us tell them tomorrow?"

"The truth, in the order it actually happened, with a plan attached that makes it structurally harder for this specific failure to recur. Not easier to explain. Harder to repeat." She let that sit. "I understand that's a worse headline than a name. I don't think it's a worse outcome, and I was hired to find you the outcome, not the headline."

"You were hired," the silver-haired director said, quietter now, "at Ms Ashworth's discretion, and that discretion can be revisited, Ms Reyes, if the board concludes it isn't serving us."

"It can," Maya agreed, and was faintly surprised, examining the feeling, to find she meant it steadily rather than defiantly. "If you replace me this week, whoever you bring in next will find exactly what I've found, because it's sitting in your own systems, dated and timestamped, and it isn't going anywhere. The only thing you'll have spent is another week, and I don't think you have one to spend twice."

Ashworth, who had said almost nothing since the meeting opened, finally spoke. "Give her the four days, David. If she hasn't got us something by Friday, we revisit." Not warmth, exactly — Maya doubted Ashworth had much warmth to spend on anyone this week — but something adjacent to it, the specific solidarity of one person recognising another doing arithmetic they also found genuinely uncomfortable.

The silver-haired director didn't look satisfied. He looked, Maya thought, like a man filing away a decision he intended to revisit regardless of what Friday brought.

"Friday," he said. "Not a day past it."

She walked out with four days left instead of six — two more gone, spent on nothing but defending her own right to keep looking — and understood, with the flat clarity of someone too tired to dress the thought up, that she had just risked the only leverage she had in this building on a bet that the truth would be persuasive enough, in time, to outrun its own inconvenience.

She had no idea yet whether that bet would pay off. She only knew she'd have made the same one again.
